TABLE B -- Finite-difference numerical accuracy and estimator variance
=====================================================================

B.1  FD ACCURACY vs STEP SIZE
-----------------------------
Ground truth = exact autograd directional derivative on the same inputs.
Frozen late `direct` checkpoint 2825000.pt, K=4, TF32 OFF, FD subtraction in
FP32 or better. Three independent jobs agree.

  eps_fd      1e-4     3e-4     1e-3     3e-3     1e-2     3e-2
  rel RMSE   6.1e-3   2.8e-3   6.3e-4   2.1e-4   1.6e-4   1.2e-3

  corr 1.00000 throughout; zero non-finite; cancellation ratio <= 4e-3.

Textbook U-curve with a broad plateau over 1e-3 .. 1e-2 -- 4-5 significant
digits at the energy scale training actually reaches. Per the pre-registered
rule ("do not choose the smallest h; choose the stable plateau") the operating
point is eps_fd = 1e-3.

VERDICT: **FD numerics are NOT the risk.** The directional derivative of the
scalar potential is recovered to ~4 significant digits at real B/2 scale.


B.2  FD ESTIMATOR VARIANCE
--------------------------
B=8, eps=1e-3, n=64 minibatches. "noise scale" = gradient-noise scale;
"mean pairwise cos" = cosine between per-minibatch gradient estimates.

  arm                 noise scale   mean pairwise cos   s/step   peak mem
  G  exact                  0.203               0.856     0.16    6.9 GiB
  A  action exact           0.341               0.768     0.23    9.9 GiB
  D  FD K=1                 6.30                0.124     0.14    6.3 GiB
  D  FD K=4                 1.95                0.333     0.30   16.6 GiB

VERDICT: **FD VARIANCE is the risk, and it is structural, not a tuning
artifact.**

This is worth stating precisely, because the obvious fix does not work. The
directional estimator is

    g_hat = u * [phi(x + h u) - phi(x - h u)] / (2h),    E[g_hat] = u u^T grad phi

Its dominant variance is the variance of u u^T g over the Rademacher direction
u, which is **independent of h**. So enlarging h -- even though the accuracy
plateau in B.1 would permit 1e-2, and rel RMSE there is actually BETTER than at
1e-3 (1.6e-4 vs 6.3e-4) -- does NOT reduce the noise scale. Float-cancellation
noise is already negligible (cancel ratio <= 4e-3); it is not what dominates.

The variance is intrinsic to probing a d = 4*32*32 = 4096-dimensional gradient
with K directions per step: one Rademacher probe recovers a rank-1 projection.
The only lever is K, and it pays the expected price -- K=4 cuts the noise scale
3.2x (6.30 -> 1.95) and raises pairwise cosine 2.7x (0.124 -> 0.333), but costs
2.1x the wall clock and 2.6x the peak memory.

CROSS-REFERENCE (a first-class negative result for FD): at 4-GPU DDP scale the
cost ordering INVERTS relative to this single-GPU microbenchmark. FD costs 2.7x
(K=1) and 6.6x (K=4) the vector arm's wall clock, where the microbenchmark above
shows D K=1 as the CHEAPEST arm per step (0.14 s/step vs G's 0.16). Single-GPU
per-step timings do not predict DDP wall clock for these arms.

CONSEQUENCE observed in Phase II-B: D K=1 plateaus at target cosine ~0.60 while
G reaches 0.7046 and V 0.7081 (914 records -- a variance ceiling, not slow
convergence), and D's median gradient norm sits at ~26x V's. See TABLE_C.
